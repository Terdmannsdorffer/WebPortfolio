A heart is a pumpmade of fibres.

Its muscle cells run in sheets that wind around the ventricles. When they shorten together the wall thickens, the base pulls down toward the apex and the whole organ wrings itself out. The light that crosses it before each beat is the electrical wave that sets it off.

+60° inside,−60° outside.

Through the thickness of the wall the fibre angle turns by about 120 degrees. The lines on the right are traced through the fibre field of this exact mesh, the one a simulation of it would use. My MSc at PUC Chile, with Francisco Sahli, is about cardiac fibre mechanics with physics-informed networks.

From imagesto motion.

In Graz, from February to August 2026, I worked with Federica Caforio’s group on PINN architectures that estimate how the heart moves, in JAX. We still work together. This is the kind of data it starts from: a cine MRI, four chambers in one slice.

About

I got into machine learning through scientific computing, and most of what I’ve done since has been about making neural networks respect the physics they approximate.

My undergraduate thesis at Universidad de los Andes compressed physics-informed networks (distillation, pruning, quantization) for non-Newtonian flow; the manuscript is in preparation. Since 2025 I’ve been doing an MSc at PUC Chile with Francisco Sahli, which took me to Federica Caforio’s group in Graz for the first half of 2026.

The rest of the time I build things to find out whether they work: the demos above, a series of language models under 16 MB, a file-transfer system that is still running at a mining company.

I’m open to ML and AI roles, remote or on-site: part-time while I finish the MSc, full-time after. Spanish is my first language and English my second; my German is just starting.
